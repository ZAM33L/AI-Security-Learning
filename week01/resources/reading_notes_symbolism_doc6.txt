Reading Notes: Symbolism and Author Intent Across Dystopian Classics

These notes explore how each author uses symbolism to reinforce the central warning of their novel, and how personal reading experience shapes interpretation over time.

Dystopian fiction often relies on small, recurring symbols to carry large ideas — a phoenix, a glass paperweight, a bottle of soma, a pair of rats. These objects function as anchors for the reader, giving abstract fears about power and control something concrete to hold onto.

It's also worth noting how personal context shapes interpretation. A reader encountering these novels during a period of political upheaval will likely draw very different conclusions than a reader encountering them purely as historical curiosities. This is part of why these books remain widely taught and re-read across generations — each era finds a new angle of relevance in them.

Finally, it's interesting to consider which of these novels feels most "prescient" today. Some readers point to the surveillance themes in 1984, others to the consumerist pacification in Brave New World, and others to the propaganda and rewriting of history common to both Animal Farm and 1984. There's no single right answer — it likely says as much about the reader's own present moment as it does about the books themselves.
